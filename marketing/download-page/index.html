Period diary

Know when your period is coming

Tap once when it starts. Sakhi counts the days and shows the range your next period will likely fall in, worked out from your own last cycles. Cramps, spotting, mood, safed pani (white discharge): each one goes on the calendar in a tap.

  • A countdown on Home, with the likely range behind it
  • A calendar that marks bleeding, pain, mood and notes in colour
  • Trends that tell you when your cycles start to change

The dates are estimates from your diary. They are not a way to avoid pregnancy, and Sakhi says so right on the screen.

Pregnancy and after birth

Week by week, in sizes you know

At 24 weeks your baby weighs about as much as a small phool gobhi (cauliflower). Sakhi shows your week, your due date and the check-ups that are due next. Tick your iron tablet each day, and keep your PMMVY and JSY claims on track.

Once your baby is born, Sakhi moves on to your 6-week check and your baby's vaccines.

Report for your doctor

Walk into the clinic with your dates ready

Every doctor asks the same things. When did your last period start? How many days between them? Sakhi puts your answers on one screen, in English or Hindi.

Show it on your phone, make the letters big for the doctor, or save it as a PDF and send it to them.

Install

Install Sakhi in four steps

Sakhi isn't on the Play Store yet, so Android asks a couple of extra questions the first time. Here's what to tap.

Download the file

Tap Download for Android on this page. Or message us on WhatsApp and we'll send you the file.

Allow the install

Android asks once whether Chrome or WhatsApp may install apps. Tap Settings, turn on Allow from this source, then go back.

If Play Protect asks

Tap More details, then Install anyway. Android shows this for any app that doesn't come from the Play Store.

Open and sign up

Choose your language and sign up with a code sent to your phone by WhatsApp or SMS. It takes about two minutes.

Getting a newer version? Install it over the old one. Don't uninstall Sakhi first, because uninstalling deletes your diary.

Questions

Before you download

Is Sakhi really free?

Yes. The diary, the guides, the reminders and the doctor's report are free, and the app has no ads. Your first 20-minute call with a Qura doctor is free too. Tests and treatment plans cost money.

Can Qura read my diary?

No. Your diary is saved only on your phone. If you ask a Qura doctor to call you, you choose which parts to share, and you see them before you send.

Will Sakhi tell me if I have PCOS?

No, Sakhi doesn't diagnose. It shows you what your diary says and tells you when it's time to see a doctor. The diagnosis comes from the doctor who examines you.

Can I use the period dates to avoid pregnancy?

No. The dates are estimates, and they don't work as contraception. If you want a method, Care explains your options and a doctor can help you choose one.

Does it work without internet?

Yes, once you've signed up. Your diary, the guides, reminders and the list of nearby health centres all work offline. Signing up and asking for a doctor's call need internet.

Is Sakhi for girls under 18?

The diary is for women 18 and over. Anyone can read the safety guide and the Learn pages. Childline 1098 is free for anyone under 18, day and night.

I have an iPhone. Can I use Sakhi?

Not yet. Sakhi works on Android phones for now.
