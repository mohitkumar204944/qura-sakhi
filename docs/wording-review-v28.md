# New wording in Version 28, for the doctor to check (22 Oct 2026)

**Status, 23 Oct 2026:** Mohit: "doctor reviewed and approved". He sent this after being asked for the doctor's review of
sections 8 and 9 (and, before that, 6 to 9) ahead of the pilot. It's his word; the sign-off itself isn't in the repo.
Wording added after this note needs its own review.

Version 28 adds four features:
- **Iron tablets:** a daily tick, a reminder and help.
- **Free check-ups for you:** a Home card for women aged 30 to 65.
- **My ASHA or ANM:** her number, a one-tap call and a summary to show her.
- **Do it online:** the government's own websites.

Mohit's rule is that new health wording needs a doctor's review before it ships. So this file lists every new sentence a
woman will read, in English and Hindi. Lines already verified on 20 Oct are reused word for word, and section 2 names where
each one comes from. Only sections 1 and 3 need a fresh look.

## 8. Version 31: fertile days, the chance of pregnancy, the countdown and the verdict (please check all of it)

Mohit asked for these on 23 Oct after feedback that another tracker's views help. Sakhi's earlier rule was never to predict
fertile days, so some doctor-checked lines that promised that had to change. They are listed first. Until this section is

and demo APKs show them.

**How the estimates are made** (please confirm the medicine, not just the words):
*Corrected in Version 32 (23 Oct, field-test audit 2.1): Version 31 used "the NHS gives 12 to 16 days" and 5 days for sperm.
The NHS page says ovulation comes "around 10 to 16 days before the next period" and that sperm can survive "for up to 7 days".
The rules below are the corrected ones.*
- **Ovulation:** most likely 14 days before her predicted next period. The NHS gives 10 to 16 days; our library article cites
  about 12 on average, from 7 to 17.
- **Fertile window:** the 6 days ending on ovulation (Wilcox 1995, cited in the library).
- **"Highest chance":** the 3 days ending on ovulation.
- **"Possible fertile days":** from 7 days before the earliest possible ovulation (16 days before her shortest gap) to the day
  after the latest (10 days before her longest), from her last 6 gaps.
- **Chance of pregnancy from sex without a method:** high or medium; on other days no chance is shown at all. Version 32 dropped
  the "low chance, not zero" label, which can read as a safe day. No day is ever called non-fertile or safe.
- **When it shows (Version 32):**
  - only while her stage is "Trying for a baby";
  - after 6 gaps of 21 to 35 days that vary by 7 days or less, at any age;
  - age 18 to 44;
  - not pregnant or in the year after a birth;
  - no hormones, and no method that stops ovulation or pregnancy;
  - never once her period is due or late.

### 12.2 The weekly target (numbers, please check)

- **Active minutes a week** build up from 50 ("hardly any exercise"), 75 ("some days") or 120 ("most days") to 150 by week 3 to
  11.
- With "habits and weight" the target goes on to 250, as the guideline advises for modest weight loss. It stays at 150 if her
  BMI is already under 23.
- **Today's suggestion** is a fifth of the week's minutes, at least 10. There are 2 strength days a week, not one after the
  other; a beginner has 1 in week 1.
- **Walking:** "Brisk walking is the easiest way to add minutes: fast enough that you can talk, but not sing."
- **Sun salutation:** offered from week 3 (week 5 for a beginner), never with back pain. 3 rounds, rising to 6 by week 10, one
  slow breath (6 seconds) in each pose.

### 12.3 Food (please check the avoid list and the six habits)

- **Plate:** half sabzi and salad, a quarter roti, rice or millet, a quarter dal, chana, paneer, egg or fish, and curd or chaas

- **Avoid:** Vanaspati and dalda (trans fat) · Crash diets, or eating very little · Weight-loss pills or powders without a
  doctor. Also: "No diet pills, weight-loss powders or supplements without your doctor."
- **Less often:** maida (white bread, biscuits, naan) · big helpings of white rice · sugar, mithai, sweets made with gur · cold
  drinks and packaged juice · fried snacks (namkeen, samosa, pakora) · packet food (chips, instant noodles) · extra salt (pickle,
  papad, sauces).
- **Six habits, one more every two weeks:**
  1. Half the plate sabzi
  2. Protein at breakfast (dal, besan cheela, sprouts, paneer, curd or an egg)
  3. One millet or whole-grain swap a day
  4. No sweet drinks
  5. A better snack (fruit, roasted chana or peanuts instead of biscuits, namkeen or mithai)
  6. Less oil
- **Regional lists:** six regions, from her PIN code or her choice (North, West, Central, South, East, North-east). They list
