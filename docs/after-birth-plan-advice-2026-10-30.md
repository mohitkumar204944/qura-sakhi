# I had a baby: how the custom plan differs and how it is personalised (advice, 30 Oct 2026)

Mohit, 30 Oct: "yes start with the I had a baby plan" (after "We have 6 stages i believe i built plans for first three").
Advice only, nothing built. It starts from the researched design in `docs/plans-design-and-build-2026-10-24-programmes.md`,
section 7 ("After the birth"), and brings it up to the rules set since: doctor first (Versions 39.28 and 39.29), the
minimalist baseline (CLAUDE.md, Version 39.27), what Getting ready (39.32) and My pregnancy (39.34 to 39.40) taught, the 3D
figure (39.39) and "app size is not a barrier".

## 1. The short answer

The first three plans each lead somewhere: habits for her cycle, ready for a pregnancy, a safe pregnancy to the birth.
After the birth the plan follows her body back, day by day from the birth to her baby's first birthday. The first 6 weeks
are about healing and safety: one short check each evening that knows the signs that can't wait, feeding, rest, and a
pelvic floor squeeze during a feed. Moving more opens only when her body says so, after her 6-week check or her doctor's
OK. So it is personalised mainly by the days since the birth, how the baby was born, how she feeds, what her pregnancy
held and where she gets care, not by a version chosen once at the start.

Today's recovery plan (`web/progs/v1.js`, `post` version 1) is a light plan: up to 3 rows from the recovery check (to day
41), one goal step (a walk after the first weeks, the feeding page, the calm or sleep routine), breastfeeding food every 2
days and the pelvic floor, plus the 6-week check link once. It has no lessons, no moves beyond the walk, no mood question,
no "back to strong" after the 6-week check, and no path if the baby dies. The new plan would be `post` version 2; women on
version 1 keep it unless they choose the new one ("Your plan has changed", Version 39.5).

## 4. The minimalist baseline, applied

- **Day 1:** at most 3 rows: the recovery check (in the evening), one pelvic floor set, her goal's step. The walk joins on
  day 14 as a 4th row 3 days a week (4 is the most on any day). The day's dated item (an ASHA day, the 6-week check, the
  contraception card, a BP check, a lesson) is the one Sakhi says card.
- **No new screen before her first step.** Everything the plan uses is already held: the birth date, the wound answer,
  feeding, her pregnancy's answers, her care route, her goal, My food. The one new question ("Only breast milk?") comes on
  day 21, the day it changes something, and needs no answer.
- **Her evening:** to day 41 one ring, the recovery check at her evening time (it replaces "How did today go?" then); her
  tablets at their own times; the walk nudge only on walk days; her 6-week check on its day. No wind-down ring and no
  bedtime: night feeds make a bedtime target unkind. Wind down stays a tool for the sleep goal. Pregnancy and birth words
  never reach the lock screen.
- **Read once, then folded:** how it works and the lessons fold after week 1. The signs that can't wait never fold.
- **One home for each fact:** the recovery check stays on its page, the vaccines and ASHA days in Check-ups and vaccines,
  the tablet days in her iron record, contraception on its page. My plan points to them and doesn't copy them.
- **No new tours or celebrations.** A missed day is never a broken streak, and a hard night is never a failure.
- **Measure it:** taps for joining and her day with `web/test/taps/`, before and after.

## 5. Safety first: what must exist before the plan

As with My pregnancy, the safety layer is built first (the roadmap's Version 40 for this stage):
1. **"My baby has died"** (the 24 Oct review's blocker for this plan). One tap on the after-birth pages and in the stage
   sheet. It stops every baby item at once (feeding, LAM, newborn signs, vaccine reminders, the baby's age on Today) and
   keeps what is hers: the recovery check to day 41 without baby words, the 6-week check, contraception, and grief support
   from `loss.js`. Words by the psychologist and the obstetrician.
2. **The signs that can't wait,** for her and for the newborn in week 1: the recovery check's 12 signs already have their
   lines and Call buttons (Version 39.27). Added: fever with feeling very unwell (sepsis) and severe belly pain as "now",
   and the newborn's danger signs on days 1 to 7.
3. **Her mood:** about 1 in 5 Indian mothers has depression after a birth (22% pooled, 19% after the first 2 weeks;
   Upadhyay 2017). A weekly "How are you in yourself?" from week 1 (inside the recovery check to day 41), with a crisis card
   (Tele-MANAS 14416, 112; 181 if someone at home hurts or scares her), and a card for her family on the signs of psychosis
   in week 1. It ships only after the psychologist's review; until then the existing mood page and Help carry it.
4. **A BP check and the sugar test** from her pregnancy's own answers, on their days.

## 6. What it reuses and what is new

- **Reuse:** `post.js` (the recovery check and its 12 signs, the normal-bleeding strip, the mood page, feeding guides and
  the feed log, contraception after the birth and LAM, the pelvic floor trainer and belly gap check, ASHA days and the
  vaccine dates, the 6-week check and its list), `iron.js` (tablets as her doctor prescribed), food while she breastfeeds
  (Version 39.9), Calm breath, `relief-sleep`, Wind down, Strength at home, the walk, the 3D figure's pipeline, `loss.js`,
  and "Bleeding after the birth" opening the recovery check (Version 39.18).
- **New:** `post` version 2 with its phases; Gentle recovery (5 minutes, with a C-section version) and Core and posture (8
  minutes), about 10 new 3D poses (side-lying breathing, pelvic tilt, heel slides, knee fall-outs, ankle pumps, rolling to
  sit up, side-lying leg lifts, hands and knees, cat-cow, arm reach); the unlock after the 6-week check; "Is my body ready?"
  (6 simple tests, a rice bag, never the baby); the leaks question; the mood question and crisis card; "My baby has died";
  about 14 weekly lessons, then monthly; the end at the first birthday; about 3,000 English words plus Hindi.
- **Evidence:** the 2025 Canadian guideline for the first year after a birth (Davenport, BJSM 2025;59(8):515 to 526):
  light activity soon after the birth, building gradually and by symptoms to at least 120 minutes a week, daily pelvic
  floor training, no medical clearance needed for light activity. WHO 2022 (positive postnatal experience): tell every woman
  about pelvic floor problems; training for women who leak (routine training to prevent leaks is not recommended, very low
  certainty). A belly gap in about 60% at 6 weeks and a third at a year (Sperstad 2016). No running or jumping before about
  3 months (Goom 2019). India's home-based newborn care visit days (in `post.js`). Iron as her doctor prescribed (Anemia
  Mukt Bharat advises 180 days after the birth; Sakhi shows no dose words).
- **Reviewers:** an obstetrician-gynaecologist (every line, the C-section branch, gentle moves from week 1, BP and sugar,
  contraception); a women's-health physiotherapist (every pose, the readiness tests, pelvic floor levels, the belly gap);
  Dr. Megha (food, habits, lessons); a psychologist or psychiatrist (the mood question, the crisis and family cards, "My
  baby has died"); the lawyer. Then a teach-back of the signs that can't wait with about 5 new mothers.

## 7. Decisions for Mohit (with recommendations)

1. **Gentle moves from week 1 after a vaginal birth, or nothing but breathing and the pelvic floor until the 6-week check?**
   Recommended: from week 1, "if you feel able" (the Canadian guideline), with the C-section version until her doctor's OK.
   The obstetrician confirms.
2. **What opens "back to strong" (core, then strength)?** Recommended: any one of her 6-week check done, "My doctor said I
   can exercise", or from day 42 "I feel ready", with two checks (bleeding no heavier with activity; stitches or wound not
   hurting when she walks).
3. **Pelvic floor:** recommended: one light daily set for every woman, and the full course as her step if she leaks or
   feels heaviness. The physiotherapist confirms.
4. **Activity target:** recommended: towards 120 minutes a week over 4 or more days, built up by how she feels (the 2025
   Canadian guideline), not WHO's 150.
5. **Length:** recommended: 18 weeks at its fullest, keep going to 6 months, then monthly to the first birthday. Or end at 6
   months.
6. **The weekly mood question from week 1?** Recommended: yes, shipped only after the psychologist reviews it and its crisis
   card.
7. **Build order:** recommended: the safety layer first ("My baby has died", the added signs, the BP and sugar rows), then
   the plan, then the routines and their 3D poses, then the lessons.
8. **Women already on today's recovery plan:** recommended: offer the new plan ("Your plan has changed"); version 1 runs on
   for anyone who doesn't choose it.
9. **"Only breast milk?"** Recommended: asked once on day 21 with the contraception card, not at the start.
